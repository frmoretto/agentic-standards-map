OpenID Foundation Agent Auth (AAuth) Working Group

Independentchartercontext entry

Verified 2026-11-23. Status as of 2026-11-23. All entries.

Status

The working group page was published on openid.net on 2026-11-11 and the group's repository, github.com/openid/aauth, was created on 2026-11-05; its first meeting is set for 2026-11-30, where, according to the agenda reproduced on the page, the group is to approve the scope in section (iii) of its charter, confirm Dick Hardt as editor and decide whether to adopt the contributed AAuth drafts as Working Group Drafts. The page says the chairs are to be confirmed at that meeting (read 2026-11-23).

Relevance

The charter's purpose is "to develop specifications for agents that act on behalf of people and organizations: how an agent proves who it is and who it acts for, and how it obtains and uses authorization across trust domains"; its scope includes missions and the person's consent to and oversight of what an agent does, and the contributed drafts include AAuth Budgets, described in the repository as "Spending ceilings for metered resources". Payment protocols are out of scope in the charter.

Committed next

  • First working group meeting (18:00 UTC): decisions on the charter scope, the editor and the adoption as Working Group Drafts of AAuth Protocol, Supervision, Events, Rich Resource Requests (R3), Budgets and HTTP Signature Keys (the last only if the IETF HTTP Working Group does not adopt it first); a call for co-chairs. (2026-11-30) [official decision or commitment] https://openid.net/wg/aauth/ (opens in a new tab)
  • Target dates in the charter's table of proposed specifications: AAuth Protocol 1.0 Implementers Draft in Q2 2027 and Final in Q1 2028; HTTP Signature Keys 1.0 Implementers Draft in Q1 2027. (undated) [normative text] https://github.com/openid/aauth/blob/be91b4c/charter.md (opens in a new tab)

Boundary

Out of scope in the charter: how a person server or supervisor decides whether to approve a request, how the person is authenticated or identity-proofed, agent runtime and model behaviour, payment protocols, and changes to OAuth 2.0, OpenID Connect or HTTP Message Signatures (RFC 9421).
