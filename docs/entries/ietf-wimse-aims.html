IETF WIMSE, AI Identity Management System (draft-ietf-wimse-aims)

IETFwork-itemcontext entry

Verified 2026-11-23. Status as of 2026-11-23. All entries.

Status

Working Group (WG) document draft-ietf-wimse-aims-01 (2026-11-02), expires 2027-05-06; Informational; adopted 2026-09-09; replaces draft-klrc-aiagent-auth. Revision -01 adds sections on long-lived sessions and streams and on interaction with policy-based systems ("A common policy language is therefore not required for the mechanisms in this document to interoperate across trust domains"; for an external policy decision point, "A common interface for that interaction is defined by the OpenID AuthZEN Authorization API"), makes deprovisioning an explicit stage of the lifecycle, and says that posture assessed at provisioning does not by itself establish that it still holds at the time of a request. In -01 (read 2026-11-16), section 10.1 says that the Mission is "typically expressed in natural language" and that its translation into authorization requirements is out of scope; section 10.8 says that confirmations an agent framework asks for during execution "do not by themselves constitute authorization" and must be bound to a grant of the authorization server, noting that CIBA "doesn't map well" to confirmation mid-execution; section 11 requires tamper-evident audit records whose minimum fields are the agent, the delegated subject, the resource or tool, the action and the decision, a timestamp and correlation identifier, the posture or risk state, and remediation or revocation events. The individual draft draft-zambo-aer1 (AER-1, revision -26 posted 2026-10-23; datatracker, read 2026-11-02) specifies "a small vocabulary for recording one AI agent tool call as a portable, independently checkable execution receipt"; it is not a WG document, and its author proposed it in issue #19 of this map's repository. Its author publishes a conformance corpus at zambo.dev/aer1/corpus.json (format aer1-evaluated-corpus/1, kit 1.5.7, referring to draft-zambo-aer1-26, 143 vectors and 146 checks, with SHA-256 digests in the index; read 2026-11-03 and again 2026-11-09, unchanged); the index shows that the corpus exists, not that the checks pass, and its scripts were not run. A companion individual draft, draft-zambo-aer1-wimse-mapping, is at revision -02 of 2026-11-01 (datatracker, read 2026-11-09; text not read). The individual draft draft-hulea-wimse-agent-mandate-00 (2026-10-24, not a WG document) cites draft-ietf-wimse-aims and defines how a relying party in one administrative domain validates the mandate under which an agent acts for a principal in another domain, adding evidence that the delegator was entitled to grant it, kept separate from the grant itself; its examples include an agent initiating a payment at a bank (text read 2026-11-02; revision -01 of 2026-11-01 is on the datatracker, read 2026-11-09, and its text was not read for this map). At the WIMSE session of IETF 127 on 2026-11-18, according to the minutes, AIMS was presented as a composition of existing standards that defines no new protocol behaviour, and a participant asked for a section listing gaps; a poll in that session showed support for a call for adoption of the Workload Authorization Grant draft (draft-carleton-workload-authz-grant), and a poll in the joint WIMSE and OAuth session of 2026-11-19 showed support for taking the OAuth Actor Profile draft (draft-mcguinness-oauth-actor-profile) to adoption in OAuth (datatracker poll records, read 2026-11-23). No adoption call for the Workload Authorization Grant appears among the subjects of the wimse list by 2026-11-23, and the second session has no notes in the minutes.

Relevance

The draft "proposes best practices for authentication and authorization of AI agent interactions"; commerce, merchant, seller or purchase text: not found in -00, 2026-10-02. ACP and AP2 appear only in the references.
